Examples / Dexterous hands

Dexterous hands

Dexterous hands handle objects with many jointed fingers, such as a five-fingered robot hand closing around a ball. The agent moves every finger joint so that the hand takes the shape a grasp needs.

Algorithm

Deterministic PPL for fine control

Random exploration disturbs fine control, so Deterministic PPL follows the gradient of the principle coefficient:

\(\phi\leftarrow\phi+\alpha\,\nabla_\phi\Big[\tfrac12\big\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol q}(s)-\boldsymbol q(s)\big\|^2-\tfrac12\big\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol q}(s)-\hat{\boldsymbol q}\big(s,\pi_\phi(s)\big)\big\|^2\Big]\)

where \(\hat{\boldsymbol q}(s,a)\) is the predicted next-step key quantity (for example, fitted by a network). This gives the gradient directly, rather than estimating it from sampled actions, and thus supports continuous optimization.

System identification

Mechanical characterization of the hand

Adroit hand

A quick, low-cost test of the hand shows how its joints and fingertips respond to commands, to support deterministic PPL (a rough estimate is enough). A new device needs only a brief test of this kind.

Joint motion

Goal
how the action affects the joint’s angular velocity
Learned
\(\hat\omega'_j=f_j\big(\theta_j,\,\omega_j,\,\theta^{\text{cmd}}_j-\theta_j\big)\) (neural network)
Probe
a few seconds of moving each joint

\(\theta_j\), \(\omega_j\): angle and angular velocity of joint \(j\); \(\theta^{\text{cmd}}_j\): its commanded angle; \(\hat\omega'_j\): predicted angular velocity one step later.

Contact force

Goal
how the action affects the force on a finger segment
Learned
\(\hat F_s=g_s\big(\boldsymbol\theta_s,\,\mathbf{x}_s,\,p_s\big)\) (neural network)
Probe
a few presses per posture on one finger (similar segments can share one network)

\(\hat F_s\): predicted force on segment \(s\); \(\boldsymbol\theta_s\), \(\mathbf{x}_s\): angles of the joints pressing it and their increments; \(p_s\): contact position along the segment.

Grip

Adroit hand

Principles

Goal
hold an upright object with all five fingers at the target force \(F_{\text{target}}\)
Key quantities
joint angular velocity \(\omega_j\); force \(F_s\) on a touching segment
PrincipleWhenReference
Close toward the grip posturefree\(\omega_j\to\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta_j)\sqrt{2a_j|\Delta_j|}\)
Hold where it touchedtouched\(\theta^{\text{cmd}}_j=\theta^{\text{touch}}_j\)
Press to the target forcethumb and two fingers touch\(F_s\to F_{\text{target}}\)

\(\Delta_j\): from the angle of joint \(j\) to the grip posture, set by the object’s height and width; \(a_j\): planned braking; \(F_{\text{target}}\): 2–6 N, drawn per episode; learned with Deterministic PPL.

Skill

Bring each finger to a target posture

Adroit hand

Principles

Only the target posture is given; no model of the hand’s joints is needed.

Key quantities
angular velocity \(\omega_j\) of each finger joint
PrincipleWhenReference
Reach the target posturealways\(\omega_j\to\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta_j)\min\big(\sqrt{2a_{\text{plan}}|\Delta_j|},\,|\Delta_j|/t_{\text{settle}}\big)\)

\(\Delta_j=\theta^*_j-\theta_j\), from the target angle of joint \(j\); a finger’s target is its sideways angle, base bend and middle bend; \(a_{\text{plan}}\), \(t_{\text{settle}}\): planned braking and settling time.

Sample efficiency

Within 40,000 environment steps

Success criterion
the finger settles at its target posture
Result
100 of 100 random target postures, joint error at most 0.13 rad (median 0.04 rad)
