Prescriptive Policy Learning
for pendulums

PPL teaches agents principles of behavior, such as how to maintain balance, how to accumulate energy, and how to land smoothly. Compared with rewards, PPL enables agents to learn good, generalizable policies directly from principles of behavior.

Teaching Agents the “Principle”, Not the “Reward”: Prescriptive Policy Learning ↗
Shan Yang, National University of Singapore

Examples

Each example shows the behavior the agent learns, the principles it is taught, and the code to train it.

Underactuated control

Underactuated systems are mechanical systems that cannot be fully driven by their controls, such as a two-link arm with a motor only at its elbow. The agent uses the coupling between driven and undriven parts to reach a target configuration or maintain balance.

Thrust-driven vehicles

Thrust-driven vehicles move only through the forces of their engines, such as a lander with a main engine and two side engines. The agent coordinates the vehicle's translation and rotation to reach a target position or follow a desired trajectory.

Robot arms

Robot arms move objects through contact, such as an arm pushing a cylinder across a table. The agent turns the arm's joints so that its hand meets the object in the right way and carries it to the goal.

Dexterous hands

Dexterous hands handle objects with many jointed fingers, such as a five-fingered robot hand closing around a ball. The agent moves every finger joint so that the hand takes the shape a grasp needs.

Planar legged robots

Planar legged robots move in a vertical plane by pushing against the ground with their legs, such as a one-legged hopper or a two-legged walker. The agent turns the joints of the legs so that the robot keeps its balance while it hops, walks or runs forward.

Principles of behavior

A principle is a simple norm of good behavior. It says how to behave, not which action to take, so the same principle holds when the state or the dynamics change.

  • Keep your balancekeep the body over its support
  • Build up energypush along the motion to swing higher
  • Slow down before arrivingbrake in time to stop where you must
  • Keep clear of obstaclesstay as far from them as you can
  • Land softlytouch down slowly and absorb the impact
  • Move toward the goal postureturn each joint toward where it should end

The method

Implementation of PPL

1

Principle

Stay upright, approach the pad, and do not descend too fast.

2

Key quantity

What the action changes directly in one step, here the velocities.

3

Reference

The velocity the principle asks for after this step, from the height, tilt and position.

4

Each step

An action follows the principle when it moves the velocity toward the reference.

Does the action follow the principle?

\[ \delta(s,a)=\tfrac12\big\|\widetilde{\bm q}(s)-\bm q(s)\big\|^2-\tfrac12\big\|\widetilde{\bm q}(s)-\bm q(s')\big\|^2 \]

Positive when the action brings the key quantity closer to the reference the principle sets.

Reinforce actions that follow the principle

\[ \theta\leftarrow\theta+\eta\,\mathbb{E}\big[\delta(s,a)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\big] \]

Actions that follow the principle more closely become more likely.

\(\bm q\) key quantity, such as an angular velocity · \(\widetilde{\bm q}(s)\) the value it should reach after this step · \(s, s'\) the state before and after the action · details in the paper ↗
